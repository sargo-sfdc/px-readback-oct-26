How PX works

PX runs a two-sided marketplace. Publishers generate consumer leads and calls, PX filters, prices and routes them in real time, and advertisers bid to receive them in their own CRM. PX earns on the spread or on fees.

The marketplace

Consumers fill out forms or call after seeing publisher content and landing pages
Advertisers call and convert those consumers into their own customers

Publishers and suppliers

Supply side, about 10 people at PX

  • Drive traffic through websites, newsletters and social
  • Sell leads and calls into the marketplace
  • Integrate with PX by API themselves, send test leads, then go live
Leads and calls

PX platform

The marketplace in the middle

Filter out bad leads and check TCPA/FCC consent
Match to buyer campaigns by vertical, state, hours and other filters
Auction each lead, where the highest bidder wins
Deliver in real time by API into the buyer’s CRM, or as aged data
Real-time delivery

Advertisers and buyers

Demand side, about 15 people at PX

  • B2B companies selling to consumers
  • Buy leads, calls or aged data
  • PX handles the CRM integration for them
  • The top priority side for growth

How PX makes money

Margin

PX keeps a share of every lead sold, and that spread is PX’s net revenue.

Publisher ~80%
PX

Zero margin with a platform fee

Buyers bid higher and win more leads. PX charges a tiered monthly fee based on volume.

Full bid to publisher, plus monthly fee to PX

Performance (CPA)

The buyer pays per outcome, for example $300 per appointment. PX carries the risk of how many leads that takes.

Paid per converted appointment

Sales directors choose the model deal by deal based on experience, with no formal playbook. The ~80% split is the illustrative example PX gave. Deals carry a projected 12-month net revenue from cohort defaults, which the team reviews against actuals every quarter.
